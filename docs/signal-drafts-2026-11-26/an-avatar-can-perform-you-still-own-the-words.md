# An Avatar Can Perform. You Still Own the Words.

*HeyGen's Avatar 6 counts, points and changes tone with the script. Its headline '84%' figure means something narrower than it sounds, and a more lifelike avatar raises the stakes of disclosure rather than lowering them.*
HeyGen released Avatar 6 on November 17, a model built to make AI video avatars gesture and react in step with what they are saying. It has also been promoted in creator videos sponsored by HeyGen. The launch record is worth reading for what it says, and for how its headline number is defined. [1][2]

## What the record establishes

HeyGen says Avatar 6 "interprets a user's script and generates corresponding gestures," addressing the repetitive motion of earlier avatars. A user can create an avatar from as little as 30 seconds of video, or connect YouTube, Instagram or TikTok accounts to use existing footage. HeyGen says the model now considers about 30 seconds of context instead of about 3, and produces native 2K video. Existing personal avatars upgrade automatically. [1]

HeyGen's product page says the avatar now gestures to match the script, "including pointing, counting, and directional motion," and that users can prompt emotion, expressions and gestures for finer control. [3]

On consent, HeyGen's technical report says that before an avatar made from a person's footage can generate video, that person must record an on-camera consent statement containing a one-time code, and HeyGen checks that the face matches the training footage. [2] The press release says realistic videos of real people stay on HeyGen's own platform, "where explicit consent is required." [1]

HeyGen also cites a survey of more than 1,000 small-business owners among its users: 71.6% said they had recorded a video for their business and then decided not to post it. [1]

## What "84%" actually measures

The launch materials say 84% of participants could not tell the avatar from the real person. [1] The technical report defines the figure more precisely. Shown the real recording and the avatar side by side, raters picked the avatar as the real one 42.2% of the time, across 270 answers. HeyGen doubles that to 84.4%, because a 50% pick rate would mean raters could not tell at all. [2]

Read plainly: in HeyGen's own side-by-side test, people still picked the real recording more often, 57.8% to 42.2%. Shown alone, real recordings were judged real 78.9% of the time; Avatar 6 with the person's own voice, 65.1%; Avatar 6 with a HeyGen voice clone, 56.5%. [2] That is a real improvement on the earlier model, which HeyGen puts at 44.4% on the same measure. [3] It is not the same as "84% of people were fooled."

## What the record does not establish

The performance figures come from HeyGen or from a ranking HeyGen cites. This brief did not test the model independently. In the interest of disclosure: Beacon is itself testing HeyGen, using Bob Burr's own face and a clone of his voice; this brief does not draw on those tests. A sponsored creator video is a demonstration, not an independent test. HeyGen's claim that results need "no tweaking, no regenerating" is a product claim, not a measured finding. [3] The record also does not tell us how audiences feel when they later learn that a video they trusted was an avatar.

## A better avatar raises the stakes of disclosure

As avatars become harder to spot, the burden shifts to the person whose face is on screen. The viewer can no longer be expected to notice. That is why disclosure matters more as the technology improves, not less.

There is a fair use case. HeyGen's own survey points to many small-business owners who never post the videos they record. [1] An avatar can help a shy expert explain a routine process. But it should never pretend to be you showing up in person.

## The Beacon lens

- **Write or review every word.** If your face says it, you own it.
- **Label it plainly.** A short line such as "This is an AI avatar of me; I wrote and approved the script."
- **Choose the right jobs.** Routine explainers and updates, yes. Apologies, condolences, bad news or anything where your presence is the point, no.
- **Run one honest test first.** Use your own script with a count, a point and a change of tone, and compare it with real footage before paying for a plan.
- **Keep your source footage and consent under your control,** and know how to delete the avatar if you change your mind.

> An avatar can perform for you. It cannot be responsible for you.
