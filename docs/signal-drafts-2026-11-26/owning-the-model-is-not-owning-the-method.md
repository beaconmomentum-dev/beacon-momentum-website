# Owning the Model Is Not the Same as Owning the Method

*Microsoft says no one wants to pour their IQ into someone else's model. What its November model releases actually establish, what 'control' means in its offer, and the small-business version of owning your setup.*
Microsoft spent November explaining why it is building its own AI models instead of relying only on a partner's. Its own announcement put the reason in one line: "no one wants to pour their IQ into someone else's model." [1] That is a useful sentence for a small business too, as long as we are careful about what Microsoft actually released and what "control" means in its offer.

## What the record establishes

On November 17, at its Ignite conference, Microsoft AI released a family of models, including MAI Code 2, MAI Code 2 Flash, MAI Cyber 2 Flash, MAI Image 2.7, MAI Realtime 1 and Decision 1.1. Microsoft says MAI Code 2 gives 98% of the performance of OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol at 40% of the price, averaged over four coding benchmarks Microsoft selected. Microsoft also says these coding models are "explicitly not yet tuned for harder problems, like fundamental science research and very long horizon agentic work." [1]

The same announcement introduced MAI Licensed Weights. A customer can take a frontier MAI model, customize it with its own data, and "run it securely in your own Azure environment." Microsoft's wording: "You control who can access it. Your data and adaptations stay yours." [1]

Behind the products is a larger model still in progress. On October 15, Microsoft said it had finished pre-training MAI Base 2, with 2.8 trillion total parameters, and that the model was "currently undergoing post-training." Microsoft said owning its pipeline end to end lets it license model weights to trusted partners. [2] On November 23, it said MAI Base 2 is being post-trained with its in-house system, "without distillation from third-party models." [3]

In a Yahoo Finance interview on November 17, Microsoft AI chief executive Mustafa Suleyman said token cost "is the thing that is going through the roof" for customers. He said a renegotiated contract with OpenAI "freed us up to go and pursue the absolute frontier," while OpenAI remains an important partner and Anthropic and Mistral models stay available on Microsoft's platform. He said "a company needs to really own and control the agents they build." Asked whether licensed models must stay in Azure, he answered: "Yeah, not any open-weight models. We have no plan to do open-weight." [4]

## What the record does not establish

As of November 26, Microsoft has not released a finished product called "MAI-2." MAI Base 2 is a base model still in post-training. [2][3]

The price and performance comparisons are Microsoft's own, on benchmarks Microsoft chose. They were not independently checked for this brief, and they say nothing about how any model performs on your work.

"Control" in the licensed-weights offer means control inside Microsoft's cloud. It is more control than renting answers from a shared service. It is not independence from the vendor, and the weights are not being released for customers to run elsewhere. [1][4]

## The small-business version of owning the model

Microsoft, one of the largest companies in the market, concluded that its core capability should not depend on one partner's model. A person or a small business does not need to train a model to apply the same reasoning. What needs owning is the part that outlasts any model:

- your notes and records of decisions,
- your clients' data, stored where you can export it,
- your written method: the steps, standards and examples that make your work yours,
- your own short test of whether a tool is good enough.

If those live only inside one vendor's product, the vendor holds more of your business than you may realize. If they live with you, models become replaceable parts.

## Keep your own evaluation set

Microsoft's comparisons use Microsoft's chosen tests. Yours should use your own. Keep five to ten real tasks from your work, with examples of a good result. When a new model or a cheaper plan appears, run those tasks before switching. That turns a vendor's headline into a decision you can defend.

## The Beacon lens

- **Write the method down.** If your process exists only in prompts inside one tool, it is not yet yours.
- **Check the exit.** Could you move your notes, client data and templates to another tool next month?
- **Read "control" closely.** Ask where the model runs, who can switch it off, and what leaves with you.
- **Test on your own work.** Treat vendor benchmarks as a starting point, not a decision.
- **Price the whole job.** A cheaper model that needs more checking may not be cheaper.

> Models will keep changing. Own the method they serve.
