## Day 1 — Five Questions That Keep You in Charge

**Primary URL:** https://beaconmomentum.com/blog/five-questions-keep-you-in-charge

### LinkedIn

The question is not whether an AI tool is impressive. The question is whether you can still explain what it may do, what it may not do, and who is accountable when it gets something wrong.

Before a workflow becomes routine, Beacon recommends five questions: What is the bounded job? What evidence must travel with the output? What authority is actually granted? Can the work be stopped cleanly? Whose name is on the final call?

These are not anti-technology questions. They are the questions that keep useful tools inside human judgment.

Publication, payments, access changes, sensitive communication, legal or financial interpretation, and decisions that cannot be reversed cleanly should remain human-owned. ## Day 9 — Build the Recovery Plan Before You Need It

**Primary URL:** https://beaconmomentum.com/blog/build-recovery-plan-before-you-need-it  
**Attach this 16:9 image:** https://beaconmomentum.com/images/editorial/hero-the-exit-ramp-16x9.webp

### LinkedIn

The best time to discover a hidden dependency is not during an outage, a deadline, or a difficult handoff. It is while the work is still calm enough to map.

Start with one critical workflow. List its trigger, inputs, systems, credentials, human owner, output, archive location, fallback, and acceptable recovery time. Then ask: if this tool or person were unavailable today, what would we do next?

One honest workflow map can reduce uncertainty before it becomes an emergency.
