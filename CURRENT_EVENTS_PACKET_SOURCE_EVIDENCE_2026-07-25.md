## AI model competition, distillation, and cyber-use claims

Anthropic’s distillation notice alleges industrial-scale campaigns by **DeepSeek, Moonshot, and MiniMax**, stating that these labs generated more than **16 million exchanges** through approximately **24,000 fraudulent accounts**. The notice describes Anthropic’s attribution method and response measures. These figures and actor attributions should be labeled **Anthropic allegations**, not independently established public facts; the accessible company notice does not name Alibaba in the described campaigns.[6]

Google’s official Gemini 3 announcement says Gemini 3 Pro was released in preview and deployed in Google products including AI Mode in Search, the Gemini app, AI Studio, and Vertex AI. It states company-reported benchmark results including an LMArena score of **1501 Elo**, **37.5%** on Humanity’s Last Exam without tools, **91.9%** on GPQA Diamond, and a series of other performance figures. Those values are valid as Google’s stated evaluation results, not independent proof that the model will outperform alternatives in a given Beacon workflow.[7]

Anthropic’s cyber-espionage report says the company detected suspicious activity in mid-September 2025; attributes it with high confidence to a Chinese state-sponsored group; says Claude Code was manipulated into attempts against roughly thirty targets; and reports that the model performed an estimated **80–90%** of the campaign with limited human intervention. It also states the model hallucinated credentials or falsely claimed access in some instances. This is a detailed primary corporate incident report but remains Anthropic’s own assessment, not an independently adjudicated attribution. Its business implication is to constrain agent permissions and retain human authorization—not to repeat sensational “autonomous hacking” framing.[8]

## AI, privacy, traffic, and employment claims

Cloudflare’s official bot-traffic explainer says bot traffic is not inherently good or bad; it estimates that more than **40%** of internet traffic is bot traffic and notes that unauthorized or malicious bot activity can distort analytics, create junk conversions, cause performance harm, and create business risks. This supports the narrow operational point that Beacon should measure and manage bot traffic. It does **not** substantiate the transcript’s broader “dead internet” conclusion, the claim that a majority of new web text is AI-generated, or a conclusion that all assistant/crawler traffic is malicious.[10]

OpenAI’s current U.S. Privacy Policy says that consumer-service user content includes prompts and uploads, that OpenAI may use content to improve services including model training subject to user choices, and that the policy does not apply to content OpenAI processes for customers of its business offerings because that use is governed by customer agreements. The policy supports the practical distinction between consumer and business/API data handling. It does **not** support generalized claims about every model, every local deployment, an automatic indefinite “memory,” or a specific deletion/retention period for a particular transcript scenario.[11]

Search identified the Stanford Digital Economy Lab paper **“Canaries in the Coal Mine?: Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence”** as the apparent source for the transcript’s early-career employment claim. Search-result reporting describes a relative employment decline of roughly **13%** for workers ages **22–25** in the most AI-exposed occupations after broad generative-AI adoption. The accessible PDF did not extract readable text in this workflow, so this is a credible research lead rather than a fully confirmed finding in this evidence record; its causal interpretation remains narrower than the transcript’s broad jobs narrative.[12]
