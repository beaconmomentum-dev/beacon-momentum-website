Five Questions That Keep You in Charge.

The question is not whether an AI tool is impressive. The question is whether you can still explain what it is allowed to do, what it is not allowed to do, and who is accountable when it gets something wrong. Before a workflow moves from experiment to routine, ask five questions: What is the bounded job? What evidence is required? What may the system read or write? How do we stop it? Who gives the final approval? 1. Is the job narrow enough to inspect?. A useful workflow has a specific input, a specific output, and explicit non-goals. “Help with research” is not a job. “Compare three named sources and flag unsupported claims” is. 2. What evidence must travel with the output?. Claims should keep their sources, dates, and limitations. If a summary cannot show where it came from, it is a draft—not a decision. 3. What authority is actually granted?. Default to read-only access. Separate drafting from publication and recommendation from execution. 4. Can the workflow be stopped cleanly?. Every recurring process needs a clear halt, a retained record, and a human review before restart. 5. Whose name is on the final call?. AI can assist the work. A person owns the judgment. That is not a limitation; it is the reason a system remains trustworthy. Use the full Five Questions field note as a review sheet before you give any workflow more responsibility.
