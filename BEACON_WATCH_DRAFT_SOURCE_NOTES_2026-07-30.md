# Beacon Watch Draft Source Notes

**Purpose:** Primary-source findings for three review-only Watch Notice drafts. These notes support educational editorial work; they do not authorize publication, legal advice, cybersecurity directives, financial recommendations, or commercial claims.

| Draft | Verified source finding | Editorial use and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Before You Give an Agent a Key** | NIST describes its AI Agent Standards Initiative as work toward agents that can act autonomously, function securely on behalf of users, and interoperate; linked work includes agent security and identity/authorization. [1] | Frame authority, identity, approval, and audit as questions for a builder. Do **not** say NIST requires the proposed worksheet or that it certifies a system. |
| **Before You Give an Agent a Key** | NIST says the AI RMF is intended for voluntary use and to help organizations incorporate trustworthiness considerations into AI design, development, use, and evaluation. [2] | Use it as a voluntary risk-management lens, not a compliance mandate. |
| **The Forty-Five-Minute Resilience Drill** | CISA tells small organizations that the guidance is not a guarantee against an incident, while emphasizing leadership ownership, MFA, written incident-response plans, tabletop exercises, patching, and tested backups. [3] | Present a calm, fictional tabletop exercise. Do **not** present Beacon as an incident-response firm or promise protection. |
| **AI Can Assist the Work. It Cannot Carry the Claim.** | The FTC is seeking comment on a proposed policy statement concerning AI output manipulation, reasonable consumer expectations, and potential unfair or deceptive conduct under the FTC Act. [4] | Use this as context for transparent representations and human proof review. Do **not** characterize the proposal as final law or give legal advice. |
| **AI Can Assist the Work. It Cannot Carry the Claim.** | In Operation AI Comply, the FTC announced actions and allegations involving AI-marketed services, fake-review tooling, and business-opportunity claims; those allegations are not findings about every AI product or seller. [5] | Use concrete examples only to explain why a proof trail matters. Do **not** infer wrongdoing, predict enforcement, or make a legal conclusion about a reader’s business. |
| **AI Can Assist the Work. It Cannot Carry the Claim.** | FTC business guidance says advertising claims must be truthful, not deceptive or unfair, and evidence-based; its substantiation policy explains the agency’s reasonable-basis approach to objective claims. [6] [7] | Treat the proposed checklist as an operational discipline for the reader’s own clarity, not as legal compliance certification. |
