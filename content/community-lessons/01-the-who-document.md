# LESSON 1: THE WHO DOCUMENT

**The one-page buyer profile that powers everything.**

---

Before you write a line of copy, before you record a video, before you spend a dollar on ads — you need to know exactly who you are talking to.

I don't mean a fictional "customer avatar" with a stock photo and a made-up name. I mean a documented reality of a living, breathing human being who is actively looking for the solution you provide. We call this The Who Document.

It is the single most important document in your business. It powers your AI prompts and your ad targeting simultaneously. When you know exactly who you are talking to, your AI generates copy that resonates, and your ads find the right people because the language itself acts as the filter.

The cost of generating content has dropped to zero. The only thing that matters now is relevance. If you don't understand your buyer better than they understand themselves, you will lose to someone who does.

---

## The Six-Section Framework

A complete Who Document focuses on six concrete realities about your buyer. You are not inventing a character. You are profiling a reality.

### 1. Who They Are

Not demographics. Their current situation and identity. What is their role? What is their daily reality? What constraints do they operate under?

Are they a solo operator trying to break six figures, or a small agency owner trying to step back from day-to-day fulfillment? Do they work from a home office while managing a family, or are they grinding out 80-hour weeks in a co-working space? What is the actual context of their life when they encounter your message?

If you don't know the context, your message will land flat. No matter how clever the hook is.

### 2. What They Tried

Nobody buys your solution as their first attempt. They have tried other things, and those things have failed. What courses did they buy? What software did they abandon? What advice did they follow that led them to a dead end?

Understanding their past failures is the key to positioning your solution as the definitive answer. If you pitch them a strategy they already tried and failed at, they will tune you out instantly. Acknowledge their past efforts. Validate their frustration. Explain why those previous attempts were doomed. Shift the blame from their lack of execution to the flawed mechanisms they were sold.

### 3. What They Fear

What are they afraid of losing? Time? Money? Status? What is the worst-case scenario if they don't solve this problem?

Do they fear going back to a corporate job? Do they fear that their spouse was right about their business being a hobby? Do they fear the next algorithm update will wipe out their revenue overnight?

When you can name their fear with precision, you demonstrate that you understand the stakes. You are no longer selling a product. You are offering a lifeline.

### 4. What They Search at 11 PM

When the house is quiet and they are alone with their problem, what exactly are they typing into the search bar?

It is not "how to optimize my conversion rate." It is "why is my ad account banned again" or "how to make payroll this week."

This is the most critical section. The 11 PM search is the moment of highest vulnerability and highest intent. If you capture the exact phrasing they use in that moment, you have the headline for your next campaign. You don't need to invent clever copy. Mirror their late-night desperation back to them with a promise of relief.

### 5. What They Must Believe Before Buying

Every purchase requires a shift in belief. What must they accept as true before they hand you their money?

They must believe the problem is solvable. They must believe your mechanism works. They must believe they are capable of executing it.

If they believe success requires a massive following and your solution relies on a small, high-ticket audience, you must dismantle their old belief before you can sell them on the new one. Map the exact sequence of logical steps required to move them from their current worldview to the one required to buy.

### 6. Their Actual Vocabulary

Listen to how they talk. What specific words, phrases, and acronyms do they use?

Do they say "leads" or "prospects"? Do they say "funnel" or "customer journey"? Do they say "ROAS" or "return on ad spend"?

The words you choose signal whether you are an insider or an outsider. If you sound like an outsider, they won't trust you with their money. Your Who Document must include a glossary of their specific terms. Edit out any language that doesn't belong to them.

## A Worked Example: The Beacon Audience

| Section | The Reality |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Who They Are** | An independent creator or coach with a small but engaged following. Exhausted by the content treadmill. Looking for a way to monetize without burning out. Currently trading time for money and hitting an income ceiling. |
| **What They Tried** | Low-ticket courses, challenge funnels, posting daily on three platforms. The "guru" courses that promised passive income but delivered more work. Ads that burned through their budget without a return. |
| **What They Fear** | Becoming irrelevant. The algorithm changing overnight. Never being able to step away without the business collapsing. Being one bad month away from having to get a real job. |
| **What They Search at 11 PM** | "How to move followers off Instagram." "Best community platform for creators." "How to charge for a newsletter." "Why is my engagement so low lately." |
| **What They Must Believe** | A smaller, high-paying community is more profitable than a massive, low-paying audience. They have enough authority to charge a premium. They don't need to be on every platform. |
| **Their Actual Vocabulary** | "Algorithm changes." "Content treadmill." "Churn rate." "Engagement drop." "Shadowbanned." "Burnout." "High-ticket." "Recurring revenue." |

> The machine makes the thing. The human earns the trust. Your Who Document is how you ensure the machine is speaking to the right human.

---

## The Dual-Use Insight

The Who Document does two jobs at once.

First, it powers your AI prompts. Feed this document into your AI and you give it the context to generate copy that sounds like it was written by someone who understands the buyer's pain. Without it, the AI generates high-speed garbage. With it, the AI becomes a precision instrument.

Second, it powers your ad targeting. The platforms have killed keywords and replaced them with context. They read your ad copy, your landing page, and your context hints to decide who sees your message. If your Who Document is accurate, your copy will naturally attract the right people and repel the wrong ones. You don't need to hack the algorithm. Feed it the exact language of your ideal buyer.

---

## Exercise: Write Your Own Who Document

You have 40 minutes. Don't overthink it. Don't invent facts. Use the raw data you have from customer conversations, support tickets, and social media comments.

1. Set a timer for 40 minutes.
2. Open a blank document.
3. Work through the six sections: Who they are, what they tried, what they fear, what they search at 11 PM, what they must believe, and their actual vocabulary.
4. Write in bullet points or short paragraphs. Focus on accuracy, not eloquence. If you don't know the answer to a section, leave it blank and make a note to find out. Don't guess.
5. When the timer goes off, stop.

This document is a living asset. Update it every time you learn something new about your buyer. Read it before you start a new project. Check it before you hit publish. It is the foundation. Treat it accordingly.
