# Lesson 3: Copy Is Targeting

The days of picking your buyer off a menu are over. You can no longer rely on checking boxes for age, income, or interests to find your audience. The platforms have changed the rules, and if you are still trying to target your ads the old way, you are wasting your money. The landscape of digital advertising has fundamentally shifted, and the tools we once used to pinpoint our ideal customers have been replaced by intelligent systems that read, analyze, and interpret our words.

Google AI Max, Meta Advantage+, and OpenAI ChatGPT ads have all eliminated keyword targeting. They do not ask you who you want to reach. Instead, they read your actual copy, analyze your landing page, and decide for themselves who should see your message. Your words pick the audience now. This is not a minor update or a new feature; it is a complete overhaul of how we connect with our market. The machine is no longer just a delivery mechanism; it is an active participant in deciding who gets to see your offer.

## The Machine Reads Your Words

Think about what this means. When you hand Meta a URL and a budget, its Advantage+ system writes the ad, picks who sees it, chooses where it runs, and decides what to bid. Google's AI Max drops keywords altogether and reads your web page to decide which searches you belong in. Even ChatGPT ads, which opened self-serve in May, replaced keywords with an empty box where you describe the sort of conversation where your thing would be worth mentioning.

If your copy is vague, it does not just convert worse—it gets put in front of the wrong people to begin with. The machine cannot find your buyer if you do not describe them clearly. It relies on the specificity of your language to build a profile of the intended recipient. When you use generic terms, broad claims, or empty marketing speak, you confuse the algorithm. It casts a wide, ineffective net, burning through your budget without delivering results.

> "You can’t pick your buyer off a menu anymore. Your words pick him. Which means vague copy doesn’t just convert worse, it gets put in front of the wrong people to begin with."

This shift requires a fundamental change in how we approach copywriting. We are no longer just writing to persuade a human reader; we are writing to instruct a machine intelligence. The copy must be precise, concrete, and deeply rooted in the reality of the buyer's situation. It must act as a beacon, signaling to the algorithm exactly who needs to see this message.

## One Artifact, Two Jobs

This brings us to a critical insight: one artifact does two jobs. The same document that powers your AI prompts also powers your targeting. We call this the Who Document.

When you write a concrete profile of your ideal reader—including their current situation, desired result, constraints, buying objections, daily context, and the exact sentence they might type into a search box—you are doing more than just preparing to write. You are building the exact targeting parameters the machines need to find your audience.

The Who Document tells the AI what to write, and it tells the ad platforms who to show it to. It is the foundation of your entire operation. It is the single source of truth that aligns your messaging with the algorithmic targeting systems. Without a clear, detailed Who Document, you are flying blind, hoping the machine will somehow guess who you are trying to reach.

Consider the elements of a strong Who Document. It goes beyond demographics. It delves into psychographics, behavioral patterns, and the specific language the buyer uses to describe their problem. It captures the emotional intensity of their situation and the urgency of their need. When you feed this level of detail into your copy, the machine recognizes the patterns and matches them with users exhibiting similar characteristics.

## The Beacon Venture Doctrine

Remember our doctrine: prove demand before you build, earn trust before you spend, and never let a single platform own your income.

You cannot prove demand if your copy is too vague to reach the right people. You cannot earn trust if your message does not speak directly to their specific situation. And you cannot protect your income if you rely on a platform's targeting algorithm instead of your own clear, compelling words.

Earning trust requires empathy, but it also requires authority. You must demonstrate that you understand the buyer's problem better than they do. You must speak their language, acknowledge their fears, and offer a clear, credible path to their desired outcome. This level of connection cannot be faked, and it cannot be achieved with generic copy.

Furthermore, relying on a single platform's targeting algorithm is a dangerous game. Algorithms change, policies shift, and accounts get banned. By mastering the art of copy-as-targeting, you build a resilient system that works across multiple platforms. Your words become the constant, the reliable engine that drives your business forward, regardless of which platform you are using today.

## The Shift from Keywords to Context

For years, we were taught to obsess over keywords. We built massive spreadsheets, analyzed search volumes, and carefully crafted our campaigns around specific phrases. We believed that if we could just bid on the right words, we would find the right buyers.

That era is over. The platforms have realized that context is far more powerful than keywords. A user might search for a specific term, but their intent could be entirely different from what we assume. The machine learning models now analyze the entire context of a user's behavior, their past interactions, and the content they consume.

When you write copy that is rich in context, you provide the machine with the data it needs to make accurate predictions. You are not just telling it what you are selling; you are telling it why it matters, who it is for, and what problem it solves. This contextual richness is what allows the algorithm to find the hidden patterns and connect your message with the people who are most likely to respond.

## The Cost of Vague Copy

Let's look at the real-world consequences of vague copy in this new environment. Imagine you are selling a specialized training program for mid-career professionals looking to transition into the tech industry.

If your copy says, "Get a better job today! Learn new skills and advance your career," the machine has very little to work with. It might show your ad to recent college graduates, people looking for part-time retail work, or retirees looking for a hobby. Your click-through rate will plummet, your conversion rate will be abysmal, and your budget will evaporate.

Now, imagine your copy says, "Stuck in middle management? Transition into a high-paying tech role without going back to college. Our 12-week intensive program is designed for experienced professionals who want to leverage their existing skills in the tech sector."

This copy is specific. It names the buyer (experienced professionals, middle management), the situation (stuck, wanting to transition), and the result (high-paying tech role, without going back to college). The machine reads this and immediately knows who to target. It looks for users who fit this profile, ignoring the recent graduates and the retirees. Your ad reaches the right people, your conversion rate soars, and your return on investment multiplies.

## Exercise: Name Your Buyer

Your assignment is simple but demanding. Rewrite your product description until it does three things:

1.  **Names the buyer:** Who exactly is this for? Be specific. Do not say "everyone" or "people who want to improve." Define their current role, their experience level, and their specific characteristics.
2.  **Names the situation:** What is their current reality? What problem are they facing right now? What is the pain point that is driving them to seek a solution? Describe the context of their struggle.
3.  **Names the result:** What specific outcome will they achieve? What does success look like for them? Be concrete and measurable. Avoid vague promises like "success" or "happiness."

Do not use marketing hype or corporate jargon. Speak directly to the person you want to help. Be authoritative, lived-in, and empathetic. Your words are your targeting now. Make them count.

Take the time to get this right. Do not rush through this exercise. The clarity of your product description will determine the success of your entire campaign. It is the foundation upon which everything else is built. If you get this wrong, no amount of budget or clever tactics will save you. If you get it right, the machine will do the heavy lifting for you, finding your ideal buyers with ruthless efficiency.

## The Future of Targeting

As AI continues to evolve, the importance of copy-as-targeting will only increase. The algorithms will become even more sophisticated at reading and interpreting our words. They will be able to detect nuance, tone, and emotional resonance.

Those who master this skill will have a massive advantage. They will be able to reach their audience with unprecedented precision, while their competitors struggle to make sense of the changing landscape. They will build deeper connections, earn more trust, and ultimately, drive more revenue.

The choice is yours. You can cling to the old ways, hoping that the platforms will eventually bring back the targeting menus. Or you can embrace the new reality, recognize that your words are your targeting, and start writing copy that actually works.

The Beacon Venture doctrine demands that we adapt, that we prove demand, and that we earn trust. This is how we do it. This is how we win in the age of AI.
