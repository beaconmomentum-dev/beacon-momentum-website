# LESSON 3: COPY IS TARGETING

**Your words pick the audience now.**

---

The days of picking your buyer off a menu are over.

Google AI Max, Meta Advantage+, and OpenAI ChatGPT ads have all eliminated keyword targeting. They don't ask you who you want to reach. They read your copy, analyze your landing page, and decide for themselves who should see your message.

That is not a minor update. It is a complete overhaul of how you find your buyer.

---

## The Machine Reads Your Words

When you hand Meta a URL and a budget, Advantage+ writes the ad, picks who sees it, chooses where it runs, and decides what to bid. Google's AI Max drops keywords and reads your web page to decide which searches you belong in. ChatGPT ads — which opened self-serve in May — replaced keywords with an empty box where you describe the sort of conversation where your thing would be worth mentioning.

If your copy is vague, it doesn't just convert worse. It gets put in front of the wrong people to begin with. The machine can't find your buyer if you don't describe them clearly.

> You can't pick your buyer off a menu anymore. Your words pick him. Vague copy doesn't just convert worse — it gets put in front of the wrong people to begin with.

---

## One Artifact, Two Jobs

This is the critical insight: the same document that powers your AI prompts also powers your targeting. That document is the Who Document from Lesson 1.

When you write a concrete profile of your ideal reader — their current situation, desired result, constraints, buying objections, daily context, and the exact sentence they might type into a search box — you are building the exact targeting parameters the machines need to find your audience.

The Who Document tells the AI what to write. It tells the ad platforms who to show it to. One artifact. Two jobs. Without it, you are flying blind.

---

## The Cost of Vague Copy

Imagine you are selling a training program for mid-career professionals looking to transition into tech.

If your copy says, "Get a better job today! Learn new skills and advance your career," the machine has nothing to work with. It might show your ad to recent college graduates, people looking for part-time retail work, or retirees looking for a hobby. Your budget evaporates.

Now imagine your copy says, "Stuck in middle management? Transition into a high-paying tech role without going back to college. Our 12-week intensive is designed for experienced professionals who want to leverage their existing skills in the tech sector."

This copy names the buyer (experienced professionals, middle management), the situation (stuck, wanting to transition), and the result (high-paying tech role, without going back to college). The machine reads this and immediately knows who to target. Your ad reaches the right people. Your conversion rate multiplies.

The difference between those two versions is not talent. It is specificity. And specificity comes from your Who Document.

---

## Exercise: Name Your Buyer

Rewrite your product description until it does three things:

1. **Names the buyer.** Who exactly is this for? Not "everyone." Not "people who want to improve." Their current role, their experience level, their specific characteristics.
2. **Names the situation.** What is their current reality? What problem are they facing right now? What is the pain point driving them to seek a solution?
3. **Names the result.** What specific outcome will they achieve? What does success look like? Be concrete. Avoid "success" or "happiness."

Don't use marketing hype. Speak directly to the person you want to help. Your words are your targeting now. Make them count.
